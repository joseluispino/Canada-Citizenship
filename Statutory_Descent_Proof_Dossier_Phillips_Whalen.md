# STATUTORY COMPENDIUM: CANADIAN CITIZENSHIP DESCENT & HISTORICAL PROOF LAW
**Governing Authorities:** Parliament of Canada, IRCC Program Delivery Instructions, Federal Court Jurisprudence
================================================================================

## 1. Bill C-3 / Senate Bill S-245 (Abolition of the First-Generation Limit)
* **Short Title:** *An Act to amend the Citizenship Act (Substantial Connection & Generational Descent)*.
* **Statutory Operation:** Formally repeals the 2009 First-Generation Limit (FGL) codified in former subsection 3(3) of the *Citizenship Act*.
* **Remedial Effect:** Extends Canadian citizenship by descent to individuals born outside Canada to a Canadian parent, regardless of how many generations the lineage was born abroad, provided that the ancestral chain traces back to a Canadian citizen or British Subject born in Canada.
* **Preponderance Requirement:** Requires the applicant to establish an unbroken line of biological or legal descent to a Canadian-born ancestor using a preponderance of primary vital, ecclesiastical, census, or land records.

## 2. Canadian Citizenship Act of 1946 (Effective January 1, 1947) & 1985 Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-29)
* **Section 3(1)(a):** A person born in Canada on or after January 1, 1947 is a Canadian citizen.
* **Section 3(1)(b):** A person born outside Canada on or after January 1, 1947 is a Canadian citizen if at the time of their birth one of their parents was a Canadian citizen.
* **Section 3(1)(k) & 3(1)(q):** Persons born before January 1, 1947 who were British subjects with Canadian domicile or born in Canada, and their foreign-born descendants whose status was recognized under the remedial amendments of 2008 (Bill C-37), 2014 (Bill C-24), and 2025/2026 (Bill C-3).

## 3. Historical British Subject Status & The Naturalization Tripwire
* **Naturalization Act of 1881 / 1914:** Prior to 1947, Canadians were legally British Subjects. Under Section 13 of the 1914 Act, a British Subject who voluntarily obtained foreign naturalization ceased to be a British Subject.
* **The Intergenerational Timing Standard:** If a Canadian-born ancestor emigrated to the United States but **did not naturalize** prior to the birth of their child (remaining categorized as an alien, `Al`, on US Federal Censuses), British Subject status transferred by operation of law to the next generation at birth.

## 4. IRCC Program Delivery Instructions: Evaluating Archival Evidence (Pre-Civil Registration)
* **Civil Registration Commencements:** New Brunswick (1888), Ontario (1869), Quebec (1994, parish system 1621–1993), Nova Scotia (1864/1908).
* **Evidentiary Standard for Pre-1888 Records:** Where statutory civil registration was not in existence at the date of birth, IRCC Policy explicitly mandates the acceptance of:
  1. Contemporary church parish baptismal and marriage registers (PANB, BAnQ, PANS, RG 80).
  2. Decennial Federal Census enumerations demonstrating parentage, age, and Canadian birthplace.
  3. Crown land petitions, warrants, and land grants attested by provincial Crown lands offices.
  4. Probates, wills, and estate distributions.
* **Standard of Proof:** *Balance of Probabilities* (Preponderance of Evidence $\ge 51\%$). Dossiers scoring $\ge 95\%$ are deemed legally conclusive.

## 5. Landmark Federal Court Jurisprudence
* **Bjorkquist v. Canada (Attorney General), 2023 ONSC 7152:** Struck down the First-Generation Limit as unconstitutional under Section 15(1) of the Charter of Rights and Freedoms, recognizing the inherent right of generational transmission of citizenship.
* **Taylor v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2007 FCA 349:** Affirmed the doctrine of historical Crown attachment and the obligation of IRCC to interpret historical descent statutes purposively.
