# 👤 Capt. John Warren Whalen (1860–1937)

## 📌 Executive Summary
**Capt. John Warren Whalen** (born August 12, 1860 in West Isles Parish, Charlotte County, New Brunswick; died September 3, 1937 in Eastport, Maine) is the **Primary Canadian Soil Anchor (*Jus Soli*)** for Lineage Chain A under Bill C-3 / Senate Bill S-245. Born on Deer Island prior to Canadian Confederation to [[Whalen, Patrick 1811-09-01|Patrick Whalen]] and [[Leslie, Eliza 1824|Eliza Leslie]], John became a renowned Passamaquoddy Bay master boatbuilder, marksman, grocer, and town constable. Throughout his 57 years in Maine, he maintained his **unbroken British Subject / Canadian status**, sworn as an **Alien (`Al`)** in four consecutive federal decennial censuses (1900, 1910, 1920, 1930).

## 📖 Biographical Narrative: A Life on the Passamaquoddy Tides

### Chapter 1: The Island Fisherboy of Passamaquoddy (1860–1880)
John Warren Whalen was born into the maritime rhythms of Deer Island and the West Isles archipelago in southwestern New Brunswick on August 12, 1860. The Whalen family was deeply rooted in the Charlotte County coastal economy—his father, [[Whalen, Patrick 1811-09-01|Patrick Whalen]], was a pioneer fisherman and farmer who operated commercial weir fisheries, while his mother, [[Leslie, Eliza 1824|Eliza Leslie]], descended from early colonial Loyalist stock. Baptized in the Anglican tradition (Anglican Parish of St. George & West Isles, **PANB Reel F-1589**), young John grew up amidst timber framing, net-mending, and the fierce 28-foot tides of Passamaquoddy Bay. From his father and elder brothers [[Whalen, William H 1845|William]] and [[Whalen, Thomas E 1848|Thomas]], John learned the exacting craft of maritime carpentry—learning to shape white oak ribs, cedar strakes, and copper fastenings for inshore fishing vessels.

### Chapter 2: The Maine Borderlands & Boatbuilding Mastery (1880–1900)
Around 1880, at age twenty, John crossed the narrow international passage from Deer Island to Eastport, Maine—then exploding into the "Sardine Capital of the World." On April 28, 1881, he married nineteen-year-old **[[Dudley, Samantha Leighton 1860|Samantha Leighton "Mattie" Dudley]]** in Eastport. 

In the bustling borderlands of Washington County, John quickly established a reputation as a master boatbuilder and shipwright. He designed and built the sturdy wooden dories, peapods, and motorized sardine carriers that powered the Passamaquoddy herring fleet. Renowned for his steady hand and sharp eye, he was also a champion marksman, frequently winning regional rifle shooting trophies sponsored by *The Eastport Sentinel*. During the late 1890s, the family resided temporarily in Calais, Maine, where on **December 14, 1898**, his son **[[Whalen, Hollis Vernon 1898-12-14|Hollis Vernon Whalen]]** was born.

### Chapter 3: The Unbroken British Subject of Key Street (1900–1937)
Following Hollis's birth, the Whalen family returned to Eastport, settling into their permanent home and commercial workshop on Key Street. In 1901, John expanded his commercial ventures by opening a grocery and provision shop (*1901 Eastport City Directory*). Highly respected for his integrity, he was appointed by the town selectmen as a special municipal constable and night watchman (*1902 Eastport Town Report*).

Despite his decades of civic service in Maine, John Warren Whalen **never renounced his allegiance to the British Crown or Canadian soil**. In the **1900, 1910, 1920, and 1930 US Federal Censuses**, John sworn under oath that he remained an **Alien (`Al`)**. He operated his Key Street boat shop well into his seventies, passing down his trade to his sons before passing away peacefully on September 3, 1937, at the age of seventy-seven.
